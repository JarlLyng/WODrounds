# Photos used as screenshot grounds

Source: Unsplash, extracted from the WODrounds brand book. The Unsplash License
allows commercial use with no permission and no attribution required.

Two things it does not cover, so they were checked separately:

- It grants no model or property release. Both photos are cropped body shots with
  no faces, so nobody depicted is readily identifiable, which is what makes them
  usable in App Store marketing.
- It does not allow selling the photo itself. We composite it as a darkened,
  desaturated background behind the app screen, which is not that.

Only the two Apple TV shots use a photo. The landscape canvas leaves room for the
gym to read around the screen, which supports the "readable from across the gym"
claim with context the UI cannot show itself. On the portrait iPhone and iPad
canvases the photo is reduced to slivers at the edges and reads as noise, so those
keep the plain brand ground. The category backs that up: the two nearest
competitors both ship plain dark grounds.

## On the site

The home page (#182) uses two more from the same set, in `docs/images/unsplash/`: the barbell
on a chalked floor (`victor-freitas-qZ-U9z4TQ6A`) as a band between the frameless pieces and
the tour, and the kettlebell (`ambitious-studio-rick-barrett-0tQLfwcr5-M`) behind the closing
wordmark. The same licence reasoning holds: cropped body shots, no faces. The design system's
`.ij-scene` makes them black and white and dims them, never in the hero, lazy-loaded with an
empty `alt`. The plank is left out because the rower's brand shows.
